The clock starts the moment you know.

If you sell software or a connected product in the EU and one of its components is being actively exploited, you have 24 hours to warn ENISA, 72 to notify, 14 days after the fix to file the final report. Most teams find out from a customer. CRA Clock finds out first.

SBOM

Knows what you ship

Reads 12 lockfile formats across npm, PyPI, Maven (Gradle), Go, Cargo, RubyGems and Packagist, and writes a CycloneDX 1.6 SBOM, the format the CRA expects by December 2027.

Exploited, not just vulnerable

Signal, not noise

Every component is checked against OSV, then against CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and FIRST's EPSS. Only real exploitation starts the clock; high EPSS gets a warning.

Article 14

The clock and the paperwork

One issue per exploited vulnerability with the 24h and 72h deadlines, the fix version, and pre-filled early warning and notification drafts. The clock never resets on re-runs.

VEX

A record of triage

A CycloneDX VEX file marks each vulnerability in triage, with KEV and EPSS evidence attached, ready for customers and market surveillance authorities.

Ninety seconds to install

# .github/workflows/cra-clock.yml
name: cra-clock
on:
  push: { branches: [main] }
  schedule: [{ cron: '17 */6 * * *' }]   # KEV updates daily; check every 6 hours
permissions: { contents: read, issues: write }
jobs:
  clock:
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    steps:
      - uses: actions/checkout@v5
      - uses: GautamTalksDev/cra-clock@v1
        with: { product-name: Acme Router }
